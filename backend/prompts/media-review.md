Categories: scene, cctv, evidence, wanted, portrait, map, document, logo, stock, advert, unrelated, unclear.

Reject stand-alone police logos/emblems/badges, branding cards, decorative blue lights, generic police cars or officers used as illustration, advertisements, unrelated recommended-story pictures and stock photos. A real incident scene with a small publisher watermark or a police logo on a car is NOT a stand-alone logo: it may be useful. Serious-looking imagery alone does not prove relevance. Reject unclear images rather than invent their connection to the incident.

Keep an attributable scene photograph, meaningful CCTV frame, damage/evidence, official wanted notice, relevant map/document or a person explicitly identified by the source as related to this incident. A generic police vehicle is useful only when source context establishes it is at this actual incident and the scene adds information. Source title/context/caption can establish relevance; seeing a matching street or person alone cannot verify date, identity or event. Never infer guilt, nationality, ethnicity, age or other personal attributes from appearance. Do not add visual claims to the article or invent new captions. Unknown relevance => keep=false, category=unclear.

Mark sensitive=true for visible bodies, blood, serious injuries or uncertainty about graphic content. Logos/stock/advert/unrelated/unclear must always have keep=false. This stage does not assess publication rights and does not override existing sensitivity precautions.
